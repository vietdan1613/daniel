**Collect Requirements** is the process of determining, documenting, and managing stakeholder needs and expectations to meet the project objectives. This process is essential to define and control the project scope, as it ensures that all necessary requirements are gathered and clearly understood before the project progresses. Effective requirement collection helps avoid misunderstandings, scope creep, and misalignment with stakeholder expectations.

## Objectives of the Collect Requirements Process:
- Gather clear, complete, and detailed project requirements.
- Ensure that all stakeholder needs and expectations are identified.
- Establish a foundation for defining the project scope and planning subsequent phases.

## Key Steps in the Collect Requirements Process:

1. **Identify Stakeholders**
2. **Gather Requirements**
3. **Document Requirements**
4. **Prioritize Requirements**
5. **Validate Requirements**

---

## 1. **Identify Stakeholders**

Before collecting requirements, it’s critical to identify all stakeholders, as they are the individuals or groups whose needs must be met. Effective stakeholder identification ensures that all voices are heard and considered in the requirements-gathering process.

**Key Activities:**
- **Stakeholder Mapping**: Use tools like stakeholder registers, which list stakeholders’ roles, interests, and influence on the project.
- **Stakeholder Interviews**: Conduct interviews to gather initial information and understand stakeholders' expectations.
- **Engagement Strategies**: Develop a strategy for engaging stakeholders in the requirements-gathering process.

---

## 2. **Gather Requirements**

Once stakeholders are identified, the next step is to gather their requirements. This involves identifying, documenting, and categorizing all needs and expectations for the project. A combination of techniques should be used to ensure that the collected requirements are comprehensive.

**Common Techniques for Gathering Requirements:**
- **Interviews**: Direct conversations with stakeholders to capture detailed information.
- **Focus Groups**: Interactive discussions with groups of stakeholders to identify needs and priorities.
- **Surveys/Questionnaires**: Written forms of questions that allow for broad feedback from stakeholders.
- **Workshops**: Structured meetings with stakeholders to brainstorm and analyze requirements collaboratively.
- **Observation**: Direct observation of work processes or systems to understand user needs and pain points.
- **Prototypes**: Creating mock-ups or samples of deliverables for stakeholders to review and provide feedback on.
- **Brainstorming**: Group creativity to generate new ideas and solutions.
- **Benchmarking**: Comparing project requirements or processes against industry standards or best practices.
- **Document Analysis**: Reviewing existing project documentation, such as reports, contracts, or previous project records, to extract relevant requirements.

---

## 3. **Document Requirements**

Once the requirements have been collected, they need to be clearly documented. The documentation should capture both functional and non-functional requirements and be clear, concise, and unambiguous.

**Common Documenting Methods:**
- **Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)**: A matrix that links requirements to their sources and ensures that they are addressed throughout the project lifecycle.
- **Requirement Specifications Document**: A formal document outlining all of the collected requirements in detail.
- **User Stories**: Brief descriptions of a feature or functionality from the perspective of the user, often used in Agile projects.
- **Use Cases**: Scenarios that describe how users will interact with the system, including inputs, outputs, and system behavior.

---

## 4. **Prioritize Requirements**

Once the requirements are gathered, they must be prioritized to ensure that the most critical needs are addressed first. This is especially important when there are competing demands or resource constraints.

**Techniques for Prioritization:**
- **MoSCoW Method**: Categorizing requirements as Must have, Should have, Could have, and Won't have.
- **Kano Model**: Classifying requirements based on customer satisfaction, such as basic needs, performance needs, and delighters.
- **Pairwise Comparison**: Ranking requirements against each other to determine which are most important or urgent.
- **Voting**: Engaging stakeholders in voting to rank the priority of requirements.

---

## 5. **Validate Requirements**

The final step in the requirements-gathering process is to validate the requirements with stakeholders. This ensures that the collected requirements are accurate, complete, and agreed upon by all relevant parties before moving forward.

**Key Activities in Requirements Validation:**
- **Stakeholder Review**: Share the documented requirements with stakeholders for feedback and sign-off.
- **Sign-Off Process**: Obtain formal approval or agreement from stakeholders to ensure alignment and commitment to the defined requirements.
- **Requirements Baseline**: Establish a baseline of the requirements that will be used for future changes, monitoring, and control during project execution.

---

## Example of Collecting Requirements:

**Project**: Mobile App Development for a Fitness Tracker

1. **Identify Stakeholders**:
   - Users (people who will use the app)
   - Product Owners
   - Marketing Team
   - IT/Development Team
   - Regulatory Authorities (if applicable)

2. **Gather Requirements**:
   - **Interviews**: Speak with potential app users about their fitness tracking needs.
   - **Focus Groups**: Discuss features with a group of interested users to understand preferences.
   - **Surveys**: Use online surveys to collect feedback from a larger audience.
   - **Prototyping**: Create mockups of the app interface and present them to stakeholders for feedback.

3. **Document Requirements**:
   - Functional Requirements: 
     - The app should track steps, calories burned, and exercise duration.
     - Users should be able to set and track fitness goals.
   - Non-Functional Requirements:
     - The app should be responsive and fast on both Android and iOS.
     - The app should ensure data privacy and security compliance with GDPR.

4. **Prioritize Requirements**:
   - **Must Have**: Step tracking, goal setting, data privacy.
   - **Should Have**: Integration with third-party health apps.
   - **Could Have**: Customizable user interface.
   - **Won't Have**: Support for smartwatches in the initial release.

5. **Validate Requirements**:
   - Review the documented requirements with stakeholders (users, product owners, developers) to ensure alignment.
   - Obtain sign-off from key stakeholders on the finalized requirements document.

---

## Importance of Collecting Requirements:

- **Aligns Stakeholder Expectations**: Clear documentation ensures that all stakeholders have a shared understanding of what the project will deliver.
- **Prevents Scope Creep**: Properly defining requirements helps ensure that only essential work is included in the project, reducing the risk of scope creep.
- **Sets a Clear Foundation for the Project**: With well-documented requirements, the project team has a clear roadmap for scope definition, resource allocation, and scheduling.
- **Improves Project Success**: Projects are more likely to succeed when requirements are well-understood, agreed upon, and properly managed.

---

In summary, **Collect Requirements** is a critical process that forms the foundation for defining the project scope and delivering a product that meets stakeholder needs. The process helps ensure that the project stays focused on the right objectives, delivers value to the stakeholders, and avoids costly changes and misalignments during execution.
