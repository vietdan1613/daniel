**Identifying stakeholders** is a critical early step in project management. It involves recognizing individuals, groups, or organizations that may affect or be affected by the project. Stakeholders play an essential role in ensuring the project aligns with organizational goals, meets expectations, and has the necessary support throughout its life cycle.

1. **Review Project Documents**: Start by examining any available project proposals, business cases, or strategic plans to gather an understanding of potential stakeholders.
2. **Brainstorming**: Collaborate with the project team and other key individuals to brainstorm who might be affected by or have an interest in the project.
3. **Ask Key Questions**:
   - Who will benefit from the project?
   - Who will be impacted negatively?
   - Who needs to approve or authorize decisions?
   - Who has expertise or resources that the project needs?
4. **Analyze the Organizational Structure**: Consider departments, teams, or individuals with direct roles in the project, and extend to those who may be indirectly affected.
5. **Consult Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)**: Engage experts who can help identify stakeholders who might not be immediately obvious.
6. **Use Stakeholder Identification Tools**:
   - **Stakeholder Register**: A document that lists all identified stakeholders along with their roles, interests, and influence on the project.
   - **Stakeholder Analysis Matrix**: A tool to assess stakeholders based on their power and interest in the project.

## Types of Stakeholders:
Stakeholders can be classified into **internal** and **external** categories, depending on their relationship with the organization and the project.

### 1. **Internal Stakeholders**:
   - **Project Sponsor**: The person or group who provides resources and support, often at the executive level, and has the authority to make decisions.
   - **Project Team**: Includes all the individuals directly involved in executing the project, such as designers, developers, engineers, and others responsible for completing project tasks.
   - **Functional Managers**: Individuals who manage areas of expertise and resources required for the project (e.g., HR, finance, IT, marketing).
   - **Project Manager**: The person responsible for planning, executing, and closing the project.
   - **Employees**: Internal users of the project deliverables (e.g., company staff using the new system or process).

### 2. **External Stakeholders**:
   - **Customers**: The ultimate users or clients who will benefit from the project deliverables, such as end-users of a product or service.
   - **Suppliers and Contractors**: Organizations or individuals providing goods, services, or expertise needed for the project.
   - **Regulatory Bodies**: Government or industry organizations that enforce compliance standards (e.g., safety regulations, environmental laws).
   - **Investors**: Individuals or entities that have a financial stake in the success of the project or organization.
   - **Media**: External influencers who might communicate about the project to the public.
   - **Community**: Local communities or groups who might be impacted by the project's outcome.

## Stakeholder Identification Criteria:
When identifying stakeholders, consider these key aspects:

- **Power**: The ability of a stakeholder to influence the project's direction or decisions.
- **Interest**: The level of concern a stakeholder has regarding the project’s outcome.
- **Influence**: The impact the stakeholder can have on project success or failure.
- **Attitude**: Whether the stakeholder is supportive, neutral, or resistant to the project.
  
## Tools and Techniques for Stakeholder Identification:

1. **Stakeholder Register**:
   A document that lists stakeholders, their roles, interests, expectations, and potential impact on the project. It helps project managers to understand stakeholder dynamics and manage communication strategies.

   Example of a Stakeholder Register:
   | Stakeholder Name | Role            | Influence | Interest | Requirements | Impact on Project |
   |------------------|-----------------|-----------|----------|--------------|-------------------|
   | John Smith       | Project Sponsor | High      | High     | Timely delivery, within budget | Decision-maker, approves budget |
   | Jane Doe         | Marketing Lead  | Medium    | High     | Stakeholder buy-in, user engagement | Provides marketing materials |
   | Sarah Lee        | Developer       | High      | Medium   | Clear requirements, access to resources | Develops the product |
   | Tom Williams     | Customer        | Low       | High     | Quality, functionality | End-user, product feedback |

2. **Power/Interest Grid**:
   A matrix that helps to categorize stakeholders based on their level of power and interest in the project, guiding communication and engagement strategies.
   - **High Power, High Interest**: Actively manage and involve.
   - **High Power, Low Interest**: Keep satisfied and informed.
   - **Low Power, High Interest**: Keep informed and engaged.
   - **Low Power, Low Interest**: Monitor with minimal effort.

   Example:
   | Stakeholder        | Power | Interest | Strategy                 |
   |--------------------|-------|----------|--------------------------|
   | Project Sponsor    | High  | High     | Manage closely, frequent updates |
   | Team Members       | High  | Medium   | Involve in decisions, update regularly |
   | Vendors            | Medium| Low      | Keep satisfied, occasional updates |
   | End-users          | Low   | High     | Keep informed, provide opportunities for feedback |

3. **Stakeholder Map**: 
   A visual representation of stakeholders based on their influence, interest, or level of engagement. This can be helpful in prioritizing engagement and communication efforts.

## Managing Stakeholder Expectations:
Once stakeholders are identified, it's important to:
- Assess their expectations and needs.
- Develop communication strategies tailored to each stakeholder or group.
- Engage stakeholders early to gather feedback and avoid misalignment.
- Regularly monitor and adjust stakeholder engagement as the project progresses.

Effective stakeholder management helps ensure project success by addressing concerns, maintaining support, and preventing conflicts.
